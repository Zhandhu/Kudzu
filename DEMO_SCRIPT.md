# Kudzu demo script

A six-minute walkthrough that shows every feature in order.

**Before you start**

1. Run `npm install` once, then `npm run dev`, and open the app on a laptop so it shows inside the phone frame.
2. Open the demo controls (wrench button, or tap the Kudzu logo three times) and choose **Reset all demo data**.
3. You should be on the welcome screen, with a box asking **Would you like a walkthrough?**

If you would rather let the app present itself, choose **Quick walkthrough** (about four minutes) or **Full tutorial** (about ten) in that box. Either one starts with sign-up, follows whichever app you pick there, and offers the other app at the end. The script below is for presenting it yourself: choose **No thanks, I’ll look around**.

**What to emphasise:** this is for keeping in touch with people you know. Reaching someone new is the second use, not the first. Every member is verified. Avoid the words "match" and "swipe": there is neither.

The demo controls are referred to below as **Demo**.

---

## 0:00 Onboarding (40 seconds)

"Kudzu is how alumni of the same school stay in touch with one another, and how students reach them. Every member is verified by the school."

1. Tap **Verify with your .edu email**. The first page asks **what brings you to Kudzu**: **Grow my network**, or **Keep in touch**, a simpler app for people who only want to stay close to the people they know. Pick **Grow my network** and tap **Continue**. Sign-up is **one menu**, not a queue of screens. Three sections are needed. The rest are optional. Each row has a box that fills in when it is done.
2. Open **You and your school**. Type a name, then `riley@gatech.edu`. Georgia Tech is detected and selected from the email domain. Tap a second school to show that someone with **two degrees** joins both networks and picks one as their **default**. Mention the alum verification path and **My school isn't here yet**. Tap **Done**: the box fills in.
3. Open **Your current career** and type a role. That is the one part that is needed. Show **Add an earlier role** and the **I changed careers** flag.
4. Open **People you already know**. Search by name or **@username**, tap someone, and add a **phone number** to invite a friend who isn't on Kudzu. Nobody starts with an empty network.
5. Point to the optional rows: **what you're here for** (nobody picks "mentor" or "mentee": the app works that out), **fields you're interested in**, which decide who is shown first and never hide anyone, **hobbies, clubs and affiliations**, and **prompts**.
6. Tap **Skip** (top right) to load the prefilled demo user, Riley, a third-year Industrial Engineering student. (Fastest route for a demo: on the first page, pick an app and tap **Skip the rest**.)

## 0:40 Find (60 seconds)

"This is where you find people. You say what you need, and the school's network answers."

1. The tab opens on **What are you looking for?** with Riley’s own asks ready to tap. (Someone who named only one or two gets the list topped up with what people at their stage usually look for.) Tap **An inside look at an industry**. A few people who can help **drop out from right under it**, each a **leaf** with a face, a name, a class year and a company. Mention **Something else**: type a need in your own words, such as "someone in consulting in New York", and the app finds people for it.
2. Tap **Maya O.** Her whole profile opens as a **card floating over the search**. Point to the **Verified alum** badge under her name: every member is confirmed by the school. Then the green **Learn from** box, people in common, and her three prompts.
3. Tap **Ask Maya for a chat**. Pick what it is about, type one line and tap **Send request**. "No swiping, and no waiting for a mutual match. You ask, with a reason."
4. After a moment the bar changes to **Message** and **Propose coffee**: Maya has accepted. (Maya always accepts. Others accept about 60% of the time, and some never answer, so that case can be shown.) Tap outside the card to close it. The search is still there.
5. Type **consult** in the search bar and tap the **Consulting** chip it offers. Results **grow on branches**, grouped by city. Tap **Level** to regroup them. (Search for one city and the app stops grouping by city on its own.) Leaves are tinted by relationship: learn from, trade notes, give back. Outlined leaves are people already in Riley's network. The corner of each leaf shows how far away they are: **1st**, **2nd** (someone Riley knows knows them) or **3rd**.
6. Tap the **+** in the search bar to show the other ways to narrow: field, city, level and **Can help with**. Tap one: it applies and the sheet closes by itself. Tap the chip to remove it.

Find is only search. Requests, and everything else that is waiting on you, are on Messages.

## 1:40 Messages (80 seconds)

"Messages is who is waiting on you to write."

1. Open the **Messages** tab. At the top are **two boxes**, side by side.
   - **Requests.** Tap it. **Asked of you** are requests from others; each is answered in one tap: **Accept**, **Later on** or **Not right now**, which is light brown so it reads as a no. **Waiting on** are Riley's own. One reads "Rachel couldn't get to this one," with a button to **see who else can help**. "A request never just goes quiet." Close it.
   - **Catch up.** Tap it. These are people Riley already knows, each with a reason to write now: Jordan is in town this week, Sam just started at Google. Tap **Message** on the Sam one. The chat opens with a congratulation already drafted. Go back.
2. Under the boxes is **Say hello**: people who accepted a request and have not been written to yet. The small **cup** on a picture means there is no coffee chat with that person so far. In the list below, **Lily Zhang** has it too: they have talked, but nothing is arranged.
3. Open **Maya**. The line from your request is the first message, and she has replied. Point to the **cup at the bottom right**: it is filled, because there is no coffee chat with her yet. Tap it. Tap **Google** to link a calendar: each time is now marked **Both free**, or says who is busy, and the best two are picked for you. Tap **Send proposal**. After a moment Maya accepts, the card becomes **Coffee chat confirmed**, and the cup button becomes an outline.
4. Go back and open **Marcus Lee**. He proposed times to you. Each says whether Riley is free. Point to **Accept**, then tap **Suggest other times**, pick two and send them. His times are set aside and he accepts one of Riley's. Mention **Talk it over**, which starts a message instead.
5. Optional: propose coffee to **Jordan Alvarez**. Jordan answers with other times, to show it from the other side.
6. Tap the **⋯** menu in the header to show **Report / Block**. Close it.

## 3:00 Network (60 seconds)

"This is the signature feature. Your network is a living thing."

1. Open the **Network** tab. It is just the **map**, with nothing floating over it. A vine grows from your root marker to each city. Each vine is a tangle of strands, one per person there, coloured by how close you are: deep green for strong, pale and dashed for fading. A brown bud marks someone you have not talked to in a long time: resting, not lost.
2. Tap the **Seattle cluster**. The map flies in on its own: the vine arrives at the city and splits into a branch for each person, and a card lists them with message and coffee buttons. Tap the **X** and the map flies back out to the whole network. (If you drag the map or tap a person while zoomed in, it stays where you put it.)
3. Switch to **List**. At the top, **Camila Torres** is in a grey box: she has said she knows Riley. **Yes, I know Camila** adds her; **Not really** drops it, and she is never told.
4. Tap **Add people you know**, then **I know them** on someone and say when you last talked. They go on Riley's list at once, marked **Waiting to confirm**: reminders work now, but messaging opens only when they confirm with one tap. "Nobody can add a stranger to get round asking with a reason." In the demo they confirm after a few seconds. Go back.
5. Connections are grouped by strength with leaf icons, and each carries a tag for the kind of relationship: **Learning from**, **Trading notes** or **Giving back**. Tap **Giving back** in the chip row to show Diego, a first-year Riley has helped.
6. Open **Devon Brooks**. Tap **What this is based on**: closeness adds up every interaction, a good coffee chat counts most, and each one fades with time. Below it, **Keeping in touch** shows the monthly rhythm Riley chose. Tap **We caught up outside Kudzu** to show how a phone call or a lunch is logged in two taps, so the tie does not look as though it has faded.
7. **Demo → Fast-forward one month.** Open **Messages** and tap **Catch up**: the first reminder is the monthly catch-up with Devon, because a month has passed. The list also suggests one long-quiet person, with the line "Old friends are usually glad to hear from you". Closeness fades slowly, so one month changes little else.

## 4:00 Community: events and groups (60 seconds)

1. Open the **Community** tab. The count on it is posts from the school that have not been read. The tab has two halves, **Events** and **Groups**.
2. On **Events**, turn the **For you** chip off to see everything, and tap a filter chip such as **Virtual**. Clear it.
3. Tap **Midtown Morning Coffee Meetup**. It opens as a **large card floating over the list**. Show the map pin, the RSVP buttons, and the **guest list**. Tap a guest you haven't met, such as **Sofia Marin**: their profile opens on top, with an **Ask for a chat** button. Close it. Point to **Event chat**: only people going are in it, it opens three days before and closes a week after. Open it and send a line; someone going answers. Tap a picture to show how it leads to a one-to-one chat. Go back and close the event.
4. Point to the **chat bubble** at the bottom right. It opens to all of Riley's event chats.
5. Tap **Host** to show the form: title, type, date, location, capacity, public or community-only. Close it.
6. Switch to **Groups**. The first two, under **Yours already**, are **Class of 2028** and **GT in Atlanta**: every member is in their class and city groups without joining. "A class is thousands of people, so it gets announcements from alumni services a few times a year, not a group chat. Nobody can reply to everyone." Open **Class of 2028**: the school's posts are at the top, then where the class has ended up, then its members.
7. **Demo → Receive a class announcement.** A notification from Georgia Tech Alumni Services drops in at the top, as it would on a phone. Tap it: it opens the class group with the new post.
8. Back on Groups, join one of the **Groups to join**, then open it to see its members and events.

## 5:00 Feedback, thank-yous and profile (40 seconds)

1. **Demo → Trigger a post-chat check-in.** Answer *Did you meet?*, give a thumbs up and pick a tag. Stress that these answers are **private and never shown on a profile**.
2. Scroll to **Send a thank-you** and type one line. This is the one part the other person sees. "In experiments, people who were thanked were more likely to help again."
3. Under **What next?**, choose **Check in monthly** and submit. Open that person's chat to show the thank-you card and their reply.
4. **Demo → Receive a thank-you note.** The Profile tab opens on **Thank-you notes**, which only Riley can see.
5. **Demo → Show account-warning notice.** Show the gentle notice on Profile and tap it to open the community guidelines.
6. On Profile, point out edit profile, chat capacity, groups and events. In Settings, **Private** means only people you are already connected with can see you, and **Pause my account** is for stepping away: nobody can find you, connect with you or reach you.

## 5:40 The other app: Keep in touch (30 seconds)

1. On Profile, find **What you want from Kudzu**, the green section above Settings, and tap **Keep in touch**. The tabs become **Home, Map, Messages, Profile**.
2. Open **Home**. Two tiles: **Catch up**, with a few people to write to and a reason for each, and **Help out**, the requests from students, which can be switched off.
3. Below is **Your people**, longest-quiet first, with anyone who says they know you in a grey box at the top. At the bottom, **From your school** leads to the class and city groups.
4. Switch back to **Grow my network** to finish.

## Closing: any school (10 seconds)

1. Tap the **GT** chip at the top of any tab. It lists Riley's school networks and marks the default. Tap **Add another school**, pick one, and fill in the degree. Without that school’s email the degree is marked pending while it is confirmed. Then swap into it.
2. **Demo → Switch school → Emory.** Flip through Find, Community and Network. Everything is now Emory.
3. **Demo → Switch user** to show the alum view. Morgan’s profile shows a career path and the **Changed careers** flag.

"Kudzu works for any school. Schools are data, not code."

## If something goes wrong

- **Stuck or cluttered state:** Demo → **Reset all demo data**, choose **No thanks, I’ll look around**, then **Skip onboarding (demo)**, pick an app, and **Skip the rest**.
- **Find shows nobody:** tap **Clear all**, or remove a filter.
- **Map is grey:** map tiles need an internet connection. The pins and vines still draw without them.
- **No confirmed chat for the check-in:** propose a coffee chat in any conversation first. It is accepted automatically.
